🌟 Executive Abstract: Why We Did It

Employee turnover is not a random anomaly; it is a measurable operational failure. At Salifort Motors, human resources historically functioned in a reactive state—conducting exit interviews only after high-performing personnel submitted their resignations. Replacing departing skilled engineering, design, and manufacturing staff costs an estimated 1.5× to 2× an employee's annual salary when factoring in recruiting fees, onboarding drag, and lost project momentum.

We embarked on an end-to-end data science initiative across 14,999 historical employee profiles to transition leadership into a proactive posture. By pinpointing structural root causes and deploying an ensemble classification model, we provide leadership with a 6-to-12 month operational intervention window before critical talent departs.

🧹 Section 2: What We Did & Data Safeguards

Predictive modeling in human resources is vulnerable to spurious correlation and data leakage. Before model training, we established two production safeguards:

  1. Dataset Deduplication: We uncovered and eliminated 3,008 duplicate employee records (~20% of the dataset), establishing an uncompromised baseline of 11,991 distinct employee lifecycles.
  2. Eliminating Survey Bias & Leakage: Traditional HR models rely on satisfaction_level. In production, satisfaction scores are subjective, collected erratically, and subject to severe reporting bias (disgruntled employees often score negatively only days before quitting). We omitted satisfaction scores entirely and engineered an objective behavioral indicator: overworked (logging average monthly hours > 175).

📊 Section 3: Diagnostic Findings (What We Found)

Exploratory data analysis revealed that attrition at Salifort Motors was not an individual morale problem, but an institutional capacity imbalance:

100% Seven-Project Attrition

Every single employee assigned to 7 concurrent projects departed the organization. Those handling 6–7 projects averaged 255 to 295 hours per month (over 10 hours daily, weekends included).

Bimodal Attrition Distribution

Departures were split into two polar clusters: Underutilized Leavers (<150 hrs/month, low evaluations, administrative offboarding) vs. Overworked Burnout Leavers (>250 hrs/month, top evaluations, no career growth).

2.1% Five-Year Promotion Ceiling

Only 2.1% of employees received a promotion within the past 5 years. Extreme overtime and stellar evaluation scores had virtually zero correlation with vertical advancement.

🔮 Section 6: Strategic Operational Playbook for Leadership

Feature importances demonstrated that last_evaluation, number_project, tenure, and overworked drove over 85% of tree splits. We provided Salifort Motors leadership with three immediate policy interventions:

1
Establish Workload Caps (4–5 Projects Max)

Enforce a hard organizational ceiling of 4–5 concurrent projects per employee. Operating at 6–7 projects is a direct predictor of total staff churn within 12 months.

2
Restructure Performance Reviews for Efficiency

Decouple evaluation scores from sheer logged hours. Reward velocity and outcome execution rather than endurance to avoid subsidizing burnout culture.

3
Critical 3-to-5 Year Tenure Interventions

Institutionalize mandatory career-pathing reviews for staff reaching year 3. With only 2.1% promoted in 5 years, mid-tenure talent departure is the company's highest cost center.
